Shuffle coordinates

Move cells on the tissue while destroying spatial contiguity. This is a physical intervention on geometry, not an ARI tweak.

Each cell keeps its molecular profile. Recoverable signal stays fixed so the prior is not given a new transcriptome.

Hold

Keep domain labels

Ground-truth membership does not change. Only where those labels sit on the lattice changes.

Contiguity is the fraction of spatial kNN neighbours (k = 6) that share the ground-truth domain label. Continuous bands (MERFISH, DLPFC) can recover a spatial prior; scattered niches (CODEX adjacency 0.022) are hurt by smoothing.

Abstract

A causal law, not a method crown

The object is whether a spatial prior is warranted, not which encoder wins an ARI table.

Methods for spatial-domain detection in spatial transcriptomics are routinely ranked on one or two Visium datasets under a single clustering backend, and each new paper claims a state-of-the-art. We argue the reliable object of study is not which method wins but a predictive, causal law for whether a spatial prior helps at all. In the primary synthetic sweep, scrambling positions while holding each cell's expression and label fixed makes spatial-prior advantage rise with contiguity and flip sign, at Spearman ρ=+0.98 to +1.00 (p<0.001; a saturated rank correlation, not an effect size). A decoupled design then isolates the intervention: manipulating contiguity alone (v2 Arm B), while holding alignment and recoverable signal fixed, reproduces the sign flip on two substrates and keeps the non-spatial floor flat. Together these controls are consistent with a causal role for contiguity rather than a mere correlate.

The same law holds observationally across eleven technologically diverse datasets: spatial-prior advantage tracks ground-truth contiguity at Spearman +0.72 (p=0.012, 95% CI [0.24, 0.92]), the only significant correlation we test; it survives control for cluster count, feature dimension, and assay modality, predicts held-out datasets, and strengthens under the author-recommended backend. Consistent with a law about tasks rather than methods, no method is a universal winner: seven different winners across the panel, a clustering backend that alone re-ranks methods by as much as the entire between-method spread, and a competitive two-line neighbour-mean floor. A label-free spatial-coherence-gain proxy recovers the direction of the law (Spearman +0.55) but, once we correct for having selected it from six candidates, is a directional, honestly marginal prior rather than a significant predictor (search-corrected p=0.28). The unit of progress should shift from building a better method to deciding, before committing, whether a spatial prior is warranted.
