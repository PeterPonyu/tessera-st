Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript for publication in IEEE Transactions on Computational Biology and Bioinformatics. The paper reports a **causal law for when spatial priors help** in unsupervised spatial-domain detection, not a claim that a new graph architecture is ARI-SOTA.

- **Causal contiguity law.** A controlled synthetic contiguity perturbation (expression fixed) shows that the sign and magnitude of spatial-prior advantage track ground-truth spatial contiguity (Spearman ρ ≈ +0.98–1.00); an 11-dataset observational panel and a search-corrected label-free proxy extend the same rule under honest uncertainty bounds.
- **Honest non-SOTA worked example.** Tessera (a boundary-aware edge-gated encoder) appears only as a worked example of how single-dataset evaluation can certify a false SOTA: cross-section ARI rank 5/7, boundary-F1 rank 3, backend-robustness rank 1. We do not reframe the contribution as beating field SOTA on ARI.
- **Differentiation from Spotscape and SpatialESD.** Spotscape diagnoses the same local-graph boundary-degradation symptom but answers with a global Similarity Telescope; SpatialESD answers “no method wins everywhere” with consensus ensembling. Our contribution is complementary: a predictive causal account of *when* a spatial prior is warranted, not an architectural or ensemble rival.
- **Why TCBB.** The work is computational biology / bioinformatics methodology (benchmark meta-analysis + causal manipulation + label-free decision rule) suited to TCBB’s scope; we seek the traditional subscription route (hybrid journal; Gold OA not elected).
